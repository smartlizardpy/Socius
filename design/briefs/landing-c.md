## The single most important rule

**Draw it perfectly flat, straight from above — a plan view.** Every building is a simple flat
shape seen from directly overhead: a rectangle, a square, an L. It has ONE face. No side walls,
no roofs in perspective, no thickness, no drop shadows, no tilt, no vanishing point, no
three-quarter view. If a shape looks like a box, it is wrong. If a shape looks like a coloured
tile lying on the ground, it is right.

Think of a flat printed plan of a neighbourhood, drawn by the same illustrator who drew the
onboarding panels — not a game map, not a 3D render, not an architectural model.

## The image — `land-map.png`, landscape 1800x1100

A city neighbourhood, flat plan view, as the setting for **three sports surfaces**:

- a small football pitch (green, with thin white markings and a centre circle),
- a padel or tennis court (blue, with thin white lines and a net drawn as a simple line),
- a running path — a curving orange or yellow line threading between the blocks.

Around them, at most **eight** building shapes as flat coloured rectangles in blue, sky, apricot
and orange tint, plus a few simple round trees as plain green or yellow circles, and two or three
streets as plain cream bands running between the blocks.

**The courts are the subject.** They are the most saturated, most detailed things in the frame;
the buildings are quiet flat tiles around them. Put one or two tiny simple figures on each
surface so the courts read as in use — drawn the same way as the onboarding figures, just small.

NO pins, markers, route arrows, compass, grid, labels, text, numerals or UI of any kind.
Leave at least a twelfth of the canvas empty around the edges.
