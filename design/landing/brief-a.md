## Palette — these values, nothing else

ink `#101A2B`, deep blue `#0B2E7A`, blue `#1350C8`, sky `#7FA6F0`, blue tint `#E3ECFF`,
orange `#F0651C`, deep orange `#B8430C`, apricot `#FFB27A`, orange tint `#FFE7D6`,
yellow `#F7C23B`, deep yellow `#D89A12`.

Blue and orange lead. Yellow is the third voice — genuinely present, about a fifth of the colour
weight, never dominant. Skin tones may be any natural range. A grass pitch may be green; that is
the only representational exception. No purple, no teal, no grey UI chrome.

**Avoid large white or near-white fills.** The background is cream, so a white shirt or a white
panel dissolves into it. Where you would reach for white, use blue tint `#E3ECFF` or apricot
`#FFB27A`. Thin white lines — court markings, a net, a highlight — are fine.

## Style

- Flat geometric vector. Bold confident shapes, generous scale, sports-pictogram energy meeting
  modern editorial illustration. Joyful and kinetic.
- NO gradients, blurs, textures, 3D rendering, or isometric perspective.
- NO text, letters, numerals, emoji, logos, watermarks or UI elements anywhere in the image.
- No phone, no screen, no app interface, no buttons, no arrows, no location pins.
- People are simple and stylised — geometric bodies, minimal facial detail, expressive through
  posture rather than faces. Mixed body types, ages, skin tones, and a mix of men and women.

## Image 1 — `land-hero.png`, landscape 2400x1200

The website's opening image: **a neighbourhood's worth of people, all playing, all at once.**

Five or six figures across a wide band, each mid-motion in a different sport — a padel or tennis
swing, a footballer striking a ball, a runner mid-stride, someone jumping for a basketball,
a cyclist. They are at different scales and different heights across the band, overlapping
slightly, so the eye travels left to right. Balls and motion arcs travel between them and tie the
composition together, so it reads as one group rather than a row of separate pictograms.

The centre of the band should be a little emptier than the sides — a website headline sits over
the middle of this image, so keep the middle third calmer and lower in contrast, with the busiest
figures pushed toward the left and right edges. Do not centre a single dominant figure.

## Image 2 — `land-map.png`, landscape 1800x1200

**A city neighbourhood seen from a little above** — the "there are games ten minutes from you"
idea, drawn as a place rather than a map interface.

A loose cluster of simple buildings and blocks, a couple of streets running between them, some
trees, and — the point of the image — **three or four sports surfaces tucked among the buildings**:
a small football pitch, a tennis or padel court with its net, a running path curving through.
One or two tiny figures on each surface, so the courts read as in-use rather than empty.

This is a drawing of a neighbourhood, NOT a map app: no pins, no markers, no route lines, no
compass, no grid, no UI. Slightly oblique from above, but flat — no isometric cubes, no
perspective vanishing point, no shadows.

**Keep the building count low.** An earlier attempt at this drew twenty buildings and fifteen
figures and turned to mush at the size it is used. Eight or nine buildings is the ceiling, drawn
large and simple, with the sports surfaces clearly the brightest and most saturated things in the
frame — the buildings are the quiet setting, the courts are the subject.
