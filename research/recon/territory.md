# TERRITORY MAP — AVENZA

## CONCEDED

**Halı saha pitch booking.** Do not build this. The supply relationship is already held on two sides. Commercially, a booking-side cluster owns it — Saha Bul (26 ratings), BiHalısaha, Pasona, SporPin's Istanbul district-filtered search, Hadimaça, and above all HalıSaha+, which ships a venue-operator SaaS panel with dynamic pricing and — the decisive detail — a sub-merchant payout architecture in which "ödeme doğrudan işletme hesabına iner; ara cüzdan yok." That is a live production answer to the exact problem Avenza's brief defers ("that needs a payments provider and probably a licence"), and it is answered on the venue side, which is the easy side. Structurally, the İBB 2026 Ücret Tarifesi settles the rest: municipal pitches sell at a published fixed price (1,500 / 2,250 / 2,800 TL per hour, KDV dahil) with no commission line and no reason to accept an intermediary. Avenza has zero venue contracts and no operator product. Concede.

**Player level in padel and tennis — as a concept.** Playtomic's help centre is explicit: "The Playtomic levels range from 0 to 7," per sport, seeded by a one-time questionnaire, then moved automatically by match result, opponent levels, partner level and a "reliability" score. Avenza's 1.0–7.0 overlaps six of seven points. Worse, Playtomic already ships the profile percentage labelled *reliability* — meaning confidence-in-level, not attendance. Two of Avenza's three headline mechanics use the incumbent's vocabulary, one of them for a different thing. In Turkey specifically, Sporsepeti has run outcome-derived ELO since 2022-07-27 (271 ratings), SmashMate shipped an ELO variant on 2026-08-05, and Matchabit, Padeon and Raqet House all advertise level matching. Concede the *idea*; the only defensible move left is the rename (Avenza's percentage should read **Katılım / Attendance**, never "reliability").

## CONTESTED

**The reliability / no-show score.** Claimed in copy, won by nobody. HalıSaha+ markets it almost verbatim — "GÜVEN PUANI — MAÇ İPTALİ DERDİ BİTSİN… maçı satıp ekibi yarı yolda bırakanları filtreler… Her oyuncunun katılım güvenilirliği profilinde görünür" — on both the TR App Store (v1.1.6, 2026-08-31) and Google Play. Halı Saha Rakip Bul is pre-launch on the same claim ("Maç sonrası karşılıklı değerlendirme sistemi ile güvenilir ve uyumlu oyuncularla oynayın"). Outside Turkey, LinkedUp is live (id 6747067822, "Check Reliability Scores to see who actually shows up"), PitchPulse ships a strictly stronger version — organisers can require a *minimum score to register* — and Playo, at 1M+ Play installs, runs majority-confirmed no-show reporting feeding a profile "Reputation rating." But nobody has scale behind it: HalıSaha+ is ~1,000 Android installs and 18 iOS ratings after four months, LinkedUp and PitchPulse are negligible. Contested, and winnable on execution — but *provisional* in one important respect: the Güven Puanı is verified as store copy only. The vendor's own profile screenshot shows 177 maç, 4.0 Puan, 59 değerlendirme, and no attendance percentage anywhere.

**Court-cost splitting and in-app payment.** The mechanic is contested — Playtomic splits with a pre-authorisation hold released two hours after the match, HalıSaha+ splits per person via iyzico ("₺3.500 / Herkes Kendi Öder ₺250") — but Avenza's *price* is the exposed flank. The Turkish floor is zero: Altıpas is "ücretsiz," SmashMate takes nothing and routes booking to the club, Adam Eksik states "Ödeme/komisyon yok." The credible alternatives are subscription (Kort at 549 TR ratings, the largest Turkish-built player app found; Sporsepeti; PFM; Padel Mates; Rovo) or organiser-side (OpenSports, 3%). And the 10% is not 10%: inside an iyzico pazaryeri architecture the commission is computed on the gross and "pazar yerinin payından kesilmektedir" — 4.29% × 198 + 0.25 = 8.74 TL out of an 18 TL fee that is itself ~1/6 KDV, leaving ~6.26 TL, about 3.5%. Contested, but only if Avenza either caps the fee in absolute TL (Playtomic's European ceiling is ~EUR 0.99 ≈ 55 TL) or moves the take off the player.

**Player level as a single cross-sport identity.** Distinct from the conceded padel case. The Turkish market is cleanly segregated — thirty-plus football apps with position-based or gamified cards (Santra's six-attribute Bronz→Elmas card, Altıpas's MVP-vote card, HalıSaha+'s Altın II / Lvl 14 / 7.541 XP), and a separate racquet cohort with ELO. No one carries one player's competence across padel and halı saha and basketball. That specific thing is contested rather than open only because Join do, Sporsal, Maç Var Mı?, Sportfrie and half a dozen other 2026-vintage TR multi-sport buddy-finders exist — none above ~300 ratings. *Provisional*: that cohort surfaced in a rebuttal sweep and was never characterised feature-by-feature.

## CROWDED

**User-hosted game creation.** Thirty-plus Turkish halı saha apps, five of them shipping in the last three months, plus Playtomic's open matches at European scale ("choose between joining an already open match, or opening one yourself"). Altıpas has 10,000+ Play installs in under five months. This is the most contested surface in Turkish sports apps and Avenza's differentiator is weakest here. Worse, Avenza's CREATE is *below* the current bar: Altıpas ships WhatsApp roster paste and install-free link join ("arkadaşların uygulamayı indirmeden linke tıklayıp kadrodaki yerini alsın"). Avenza's host must make four friends install an app. Build the import and the link-join as table stakes, then stop spending attention here.

**Two-way post-game rating.** Every serious competitor ships it. Altıpas builds its card from "değerlendirmeler," Santra runs opponent-confirmed scores plus a fair-play puanı, HalıSaha+ runs four-way rating (player↔venue, captain↔opponent, participant↔organiser), OpenSports has organiser reviews, Sporsepeti takes submitted scores. The feature is free; the hard problem is response rate, and nobody's marketing tells you who solved it.

**Venue pages.** Derived-from-games venue pages are thin content in a space where Google Maps already holds the query. HalıSaha+ already publishes a Kadıköy page with 28 venues (scraped from Maps, all "Fiyat bilgisi için arayın") and SporPin exposes district-filtered Istanbul search. Court *price*, which none of them publishes, is the only differentiated field — and it belongs in the Bulletin's local strand, not in a page template. *Provisional*: no evidence was gathered on whether Turkish players search venues in-app at all.

## OPEN

**Eligibility filtering — hiding games you can't join. OPEN, and a GRAVEYARD.** Nobody does this, and the reason is visible in the leader's design. Playtomic, which has more level data than anyone, deliberately *shows* out-of-range matches, badges them "Out of range," and routes to "Request a spot." SmashMate lets you browse matches and courts without an account at all. GoodRec's city pages render venue, date and time with no login and gate only the JOIN. The entire category's instinct is to show more supply to a user who has none, and Avenza's default does the opposite in the one city where it will have the least supply. This is unoccupied territory that is unoccupied because walking into it empties your feed. Flip the host opt-in to ON, render ineligible games with a "Seviye dışı" badge, route to a request. Do not copy Playtomic's unanimous-approval rule — it is sized for a four-player padel roster and would strangle a fourteen-player halı saha game.

**The editorial bulletin feed. OPEN among match-finders, and a GRAVEYARD.** Four reasons, in order of force. (1) The attention is already owned: Mackolik has 42,171 TR ratings and is already open on the phone. Soloscore, the natural experiment for news-plus-social in Turkish, sits at 74 ratings after two and a half months. (2) Sourcing is hostile: Hürriyet's terms carry an express derivative-works ban ("bu içerikten türev eserler yaratmayacaklarını") and its robots.txt blocks ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai and GPTBot outright. (3) A TBMM bill (Esas No 2/3519, filed 10 February 2026) names "otomatik özet" and "haber kartı/paneli" as Ek Unsur — Avenza's exact mechanic — with duties gated above 100m TL / 1m MAU. Unenacted and single-MP, so directional only, but it prices the upside: succeed at scale and the feature converts into a licensing liability. (4) The apps that do bundle news into a utility (KORTBUL at 3 ratings, MYELEVEN, STAYIN, MALAEB) show it neither helps nor is novel. **Keep only the local strand** — new venues, court prices, leagues with spots. That half is content nobody else has, it costs nothing legally, and it is the one genuinely Avenza-only editorial asset.

**Multi-sport breadth. OPEN, and a GRAVEYARD unless sequenced.** Nothing in Turkey spans padel + halı saha + basketball + running with one identity; Hadimaça comes closest and does it on the booking side. Playo (5M+, India) proves the shape works in an emerging market — but it converged on adding company-hosted inventory rather than trusting user hosting, and Rovo (30+ sports, Singapore) sits at 31 ratings. The reason this ground is empty is arithmetic: liquidity is per sport per district, so eight sports in one district is eight simultaneous cold starts, seven of which will be dead on Thursday night. Opportunity only if the sports ship serially — football first because that is where the demand is proven — with breadth carried by the *profile* from day one and by *supply* one sport at a time.

**Istanbul / Kadıköy neighbourhood focus. OPEN, and the one clear OPPORTUNITY.** Every Turkish competitor is national in framing and therefore thin everywhere. HalıSaha+ names Kadıköy in a marketing district-picker, but its seed venue is "HalıSaha+ Demo Park Halı Saha, Yenimahalle, Ankara," its live district chats are Çankaya/Yenimahalle/Bala, and its Kadıköy page lists a 50,000-seat stadium among the pitches. Altıpas *plans* Istanbul pilots. Maçla's own reviewers ask it to cover all 81 provinces. Playtomic has no /tr/ locale, no Turkish among 13 app languages, no Turkish city among 52 city pages, and 0 of 140 sampled clubs in Turkey. Meanwhile demand is demonstrably there: an Adam Eksik reviewer found "50 farklı maça" in Istanbul in a year through a semi-dormant classifieds app. Pezo — one city, one sport, 960 ratings — is the shape. This is the only capability where Avenza can plausibly be the best product in the world.

## THE ANTI-REFERENCE

**HalıSaha+ (Parpali Yazılım).** It is not the biggest competitor; it is the most instructive, because it is Avenza's own brief already shipped. Güven Puanı, AI level placement, iyzico per-person split, four-way post-match rating, venue pages, tournaments and leagues, a Tribün social feed, rent-a-goalkeeper, and an operator SaaS panel with dynamic pricing — all live, updated 2026-08-31. And after four months it has ~1,000 Android installs, 18 iOS ratings, a demo venue still visible in its own store screenshot, a Kadıköy directory of 28 price-less Maps scrapes, and a profile card that does not render the trust score its copy sells.

The failure mode: **it built the entire feature list before it had one district's worth of real supply, and the feature list did not generate the supply.** Every mechanic in it — the trust score above all — requires games that already happened. It shipped the endgame and skipped the cold start.

That is precisely Avenza's risk profile today: a high-fidelity demo of a complete loop running on hard-coded data. The confirming second case is Halısahavar, still the rating leader of the Turkish halı saha social category at 154 ratings, abandoned since 2022 — and the pattern repeats at scale in Sportaneous (acqui-hired 2013), Sportyfriends (pivoted to white-label club software) and LeagueApps (née Sportsvite, exited consumer social entirely). Discovery-only products do not retain; their operators exit into venue software. The teachable point is that liquidity is the product and features are the packaging.

## THE BET

**Contradicted as stated. Salvageable only if restated.**

The literal sentence — "Nobody else knows whether the stranger you matched with will actually turn up, or is really the level they claim" — is false on both halves, and falsified in Turkish. HalıSaha+ says it in its own store copy on two platforms. Halı Saha Rakip Bul is pre-launching on it in the same window. Playo has run it at 1M+ installs with a majority-confirmation anti-abuse rule Avenza should copy. PitchPulse ships a stronger version with score-gated registration. And the second half — level — is owned outright by Playtomic, which has run per-sport 0–7 since long before Avenza existed and even uses Avenza's word for a different metric.

The harder objection is not that the trust layer is taken. It is that the four largest operators in the category looked at this problem and chose money instead. GoodRec (13,693 ratings) answers no-shows with prepayment. Plei (12,427) with a $5 fee, forfeited payment inside three hours, and account deactivation. Playtomic with a pre-authorisation hold released two hours post-match and a no-show debt that blocks the account — and it deliberately voids the match "so it doesn't affect any player's level," keeping attendance *out* of reputation on purpose. That is four independent teams declining to build a reliability score, and the reason is structural: a deposit works on a user's very first game, and a reputation score is worth nothing until that user has played five. **A trust layer cannot be a wedge, because it does not exist at install.** Avenza already takes payment at join. The deposit is already the trust layer; the score is a second-order refinement of it.

So the bet is misdirected rather than wrong. Reliability is a real, defensible **retention** mechanic in a market where nobody has actually shipped a working one — and it is the thing that stops a dense district from decaying. It is not an **acquisition** story, and the brief is spending it as one.

What the evidence supports leading with, in order: **density in Kadıköy and the Anatolian shore**, which is genuinely open and where every rival is nationally thin; **liquidity import** — WhatsApp roster paste and install-free web link join, which Altıpas has and Avenza does not, and which is a far cheaper mechanism than any reputation system; and **one identity across eight sports**, launched one sport at a time. Then build the attendance score — renamed Katılım, majority-confirmed, and shown next to the existing prepayment rather than instead of it — as the reason people are still there in month six.
